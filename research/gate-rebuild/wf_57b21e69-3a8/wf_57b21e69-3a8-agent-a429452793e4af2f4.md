**Direct bearing on the stated context** (gate = in-sample backtest Sharpe, correlation to profit −0.031, n=260, 29.2% win): this paper argues that configuration is not merely uninformative but *actively adverse*, because memory effects make overfit rules loss-maximizing rather than merely random. The 29.2% win rate is consistent with the paper's predicted signature.

**Fields to snapshot** implied by Eq. (2): DSR needs five variables beyond the Sharpe itself — N (independent trials), V[{SR_n}] (variance of SRs across trials), T (sample length), skewness γ3, kurtosis γ4. The critical one is N, which pipelines almost never log and cannot reconstruct after the fact — it must be recorded at selection time, not at entry.

Two caveats on evidence strength worth carrying forward: the DSR derivation assumes trials' Sharpes are Normal and i.i.d. (Appendix 3 addresses dependence, which is the realistic case for 200 correlated perps), and the numerical example is illustrative rather than an empirical out-of-sample validation.
