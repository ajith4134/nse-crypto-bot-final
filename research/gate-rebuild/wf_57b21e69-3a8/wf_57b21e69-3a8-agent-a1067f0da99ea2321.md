The supporting quote is **real and accurately transcribed** — it appears verbatim in the v1 abstract. But the claim built on it is a selective read that inverts the paper's own conclusion for the use case at hand.

**The paper's abstract contains both halves of the story:**
- Contemporaneous: "multi-asset models with cross-impact do not provide additional explanatory power for contemporaneous impact compared to a sparse model without cross-impact terms" (in-sample R² gain of only 0.71%)
- Predictive: **"lagged cross-asset OFIs do improve the forecasting of future returns"** — and the authors state this significantly improves *out-of-sample* R² at short intraday horizons

The claim quotes the first finding and suppresses the second. A trade-entry edge model is a **forecasting** problem, not a contemporaneous-decomposition problem. The paper's contemporaneous result is a statement about *explaining the same interval's return using the same interval's flow* — a quantity unavailable at entry time. The relevant finding for entry modeling is the predictive one, which says the opposite of the claim's prescription. The "so" in the claim ("build multi-level own-book OFI before any cross-sectional order-flow machinery") is a non-sequitur: the paper affirmatively supports lagged cross-asset OFI for exactly the horizons a 500ms/5m entry model targets.

**Two further problems:**
1. **Domain transfer** — the study is NASDAQ equities (top 100 S&P 500 components, LOBSTER, 2017–2019, minute-level). The target context is crypto perps. Crypto has a dominant-factor structure (BTC/ETH beta) with far stronger cross-asset lead-lag than equities, which makes cross-sectional flow *more* likely to carry independent signal, not less.
2. **The word "SUBSUMED"** is an overreach even on the contemporaneous result. The paper reports a small-but-nonzero 0.71% incremental R², and its finding is about the marginal value of cross terms in a sparse-model comparison — not a mathematical claim that multi-level OFI spans the cross-impact space.

The one salvageable kernel: multi-level (vs best-level-only) own-book OFI *is* well-supported as a strong, cheap baseline, and building it early is reasonable. But that is a much weaker claim than the one under review, and it does not license deprioritizing cross-sectional order flow for a predictive entry model.
